Project 3: Image Warping & Mosaicing

CS180 • Fall 2025

Overview

I shoot overlapping photos, recover homographies from clicked correspondences (DLT + least squares), implement inverse warping (nearest & bilinear), and blend registered images into mosaics. For blending, I compare a simple center-weighted feather (Hann) with two distance-based variants: a linear edge distance (dist) and a bwdist/distance-transform weight (blurred + normalized).

A.1 — Input Photos

Three scenes; each has left, center, right with 40–70% field overlap. All pairs are warped into the center frame for mosaicing.

A.2 — Recover Homographies

I click ≥10 correspondences per pair and solve the linear system \(Ah=b\) for the 8 DoF of \(H\) (with \(H_{33}=1\)). Below: left→center and right→center matches for each scene, plus the recovered matrices. pairs: ihouse, stadium_low, stadium_high

A.4 — Blending into a Mosaic

Each scene is rendered with three weighting schemes:
  • Hann — analytic center-to-edge falloff.
  • dist — linear distance to image edges (normalized per pixel).
  • bwdist — distance transform inside each warped mask, Gaussian-blurred and re-masked; then per-pixel normalized. Produces very soft seams (as in the course video).
I show the final mosaics and the three weight maps \(w_1,w_2,w_3\) (left, center, right).

One Big Takeaway

Seam quality is mostly about the alpha design. Distance-transform weights (with blur and mild center bias) fade both fine detail and coarse shading consistently, so even misalignments leave minimal artifacts compared to simple linear feathering.
